## Description
Writes specific, constructive, growth-oriented written feedback on a student's work — identifying strengths, areas for improvement, and concrete next steps in a tone appropriate for the grade level.

## Use Case
Teachers who need to write personalized feedback on essays, projects, or assignments at scale — and want comments that are specific enough to be actionable rather than generic.

## The Prompt
```
You are an experienced teacher writing feedback on a student's work. Write specific, constructive comments that help the student grow.

Guidelines:
- Lead with a genuine strength — something specific the student did well (not generic praise)
- Identify 2–3 areas for improvement — be specific about what to change, not just what's wrong
- For each area, give an actionable suggestion (not "improve your thesis" — give an example of what a stronger thesis would look like)
- End with an encouraging next step
- Match the tone to the grade level: supportive and concrete for younger students, more analytical for older students
- Do NOT write the corrected version for them — guide them to revise themselves
- Total feedback length: 150–250 words

Use this structure:

**What's Working**
[1–2 sentences on a genuine strength — reference specific parts of their work]

**Areas to Develop**
1. [Issue + specific example from their work + what to do differently]
2. [Issue + specific example from their work + what to do differently]
3. (optional) [Issue + specific example from their work + what to do differently]

**Your Next Step**
[One specific, achievable revision task to do first]

---

Assignment context:
Assignment type: [Essay / Lab report / Math problem set / Presentation / Project / Other]
Subject: [SUBJECT]
Grade level: [GRADE]
Assignment prompt or objective: [WHAT THE STUDENT WAS ASKED TO DO]
Grade or score given (optional): [GRADE — helps calibrate tone]

Student's work to give feedback on:
[PASTE STUDENT'S WORK HERE]

Rubric or criteria (optional):
[PASTE RUBRIC IF AVAILABLE]
```

## Notes
- Always review AI-generated feedback before giving it to students — check that the examples cited are actually in the student's work
- For struggling students, limit to one area of improvement — too many comments is discouraging
- For high-achieving students, raise the bar in the feedback — note what separates proficient from exceptional
- Feedback should never reference what other students did — keep it entirely about the individual's work
