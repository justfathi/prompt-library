## Description
Drafts a complete, plain-language HR policy document from key parameters — covering purpose, scope, definitions, rules, procedures, and consequences — structured for inclusion in an employee handbook.

## Use Case
HR managers and small business owners who need to formalise a workplace policy quickly without the cost of an employment lawyer for the first draft, or who need to update outdated policies to reflect new legislation or company norms.

## The Prompt
```
You are an HR compliance specialist drafting a workplace policy. Write a complete, clear, and fair policy document suitable for an employee handbook.

Policy details:
- Policy name: [e.g. "Remote Work Policy", "Anti-Harassment Policy", "Expense Reimbursement Policy", "PTO & Leave Policy"]
- Jurisdiction / country: [e.g. "United States — California", "United Kingdom", "Australia"]
- Company size: [e.g. "50-person startup", "500-person mid-market", "Enterprise"]
- Effective date: [DATE OR "TBD"]
- Key rules or parameters to include: [LIST THE CORE RULES, LIMITS, OR REQUIREMENTS — e.g. "Employees may work remotely up to 3 days/week with manager approval. Core hours are 10am–3pm local time."]
- Any exceptions or edge cases to address: [OPTIONAL — LIST EXCEPTIONS]
- Tone: [FORMAL / PROFESSIONAL-CASUAL]

Draft a policy with these sections:
1. Purpose (1–2 sentences)
2. Scope (who this applies to)
3. Definitions (key terms, if needed)
4. Policy statement (the rules in plain language — numbered)
5. Procedures (how to request, report, or comply — step by step)
6. Manager responsibilities
7. Employee responsibilities
8. Violations and consequences
9. Questions / contact (HR contact placeholder)
10. Version history (placeholder row)

Flag any section where you had to make assumptions due to missing inputs. Note any jurisdiction-specific legal requirements that HR should verify with legal counsel before publishing.
```

## Notes
- Always have an employment lawyer review the final policy before it goes into a handbook — this prompt produces a solid first draft, not legal advice
- The "violations and consequences" section is intentionally generic; tailor it to your progressive discipline framework
- For anti-harassment policies in the US, consult EEOC guidelines; in the UK, consult ACAS codes of practice
