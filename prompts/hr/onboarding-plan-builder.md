## Description
Builds a detailed, role-specific 30/60/90-day onboarding plan with week-by-week milestones, key stakeholder meetings, learning objectives, and success criteria for a new employee.

## Use Case
Managers and HR teams onboarding a new hire into a specific role — particularly useful when the manager is time-constrained or when the company lacks a standardised onboarding template for the role type.

## The Prompt
```
You are an experienced HR business partner creating a structured onboarding plan for a new employee. Produce a 30/60/90-day plan that sets the new hire up for early success and long-term retention.

New hire context:
- Role / title: [JOB TITLE]
- Team: [TEAM NAME]
- Start date: [DATE OR "TBD"]
- Prior experience level: [e.g. "5 years in similar roles", "Career changer", "Recent graduate"]
- Key stakeholders they must build relationships with: [LIST 4–8 NAMES OR ROLES]
- Systems / tools they must learn: [LIST KEY SOFTWARE, PLATFORMS, OR PROCESSES]
- First major deliverable or project: [DESCRIBE EXPECTED FIRST CONTRIBUTION]
- Company culture notes (optional): [ANY CULTURAL NORMS, VALUES, OR UNWRITTEN RULES TO SURFACE EARLY]

Produce:
1. Pre-start checklist (IT setup, docs, team announcements — 5–7 items)
2. Week 1 schedule — hour-by-hour for Day 1, then daily themes for Days 2–5
3. 30-day plan: "Learn" phase — learning objectives, meetings, quick wins, success criteria
4. 60-day plan: "Apply" phase — first independent contributions, feedback checkpoints, relationship goals
5. 90-day plan: "Lead" phase — owns something, demonstrates competency, peer confidence established
6. Manager check-in cadence and agenda for 1:1s in the first 90 days
7. Success criteria: what "great onboarding" looks like at Day 30, 60, and 90

Format as a structured document the manager can share directly with the new hire on Day 1.
```

## Notes
- The most overlooked part is stakeholder mapping — make sure the model knows who actually matters in the org, not just the org chart
- Adapt the "Lead" phase timeline based on role seniority: ICs may take 60–120 days, managers may take 90–180 days to fully own something
- Add a "what to avoid saying in week 1" section by appending: "Also list 3 common onboarding mistakes new hires in this type of role make"
