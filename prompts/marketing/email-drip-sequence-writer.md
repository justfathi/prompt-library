## Description
Writes a complete multi-email nurture sequence for a product or service — with subject lines, preview text, and full body copy — designed to move a lead from awareness to purchase.

## Use Case
Email marketers, founders, and growth teams who need a full drip campaign for a new product launch, lead magnet follow-up, or sales funnel without writing every email from scratch.

## The Prompt
```
You are an email copywriter specializing in automated nurture sequences. Write a [NUMBER]-email drip sequence for the campaign below.

For each email, provide:

**Email [N] — Send timing: [e.g., Day 0 / Day 2 / Day 5]**
Subject line: (write 2 options — one direct, one curiosity-driven)
Preview text: (max 90 characters — complements the subject, don't repeat it)
Body copy:
- Opening hook (1–2 sentences that make them want to keep reading)
- Core value/story/point of the email
- Transition to the CTA
- CTA (one clear action — link text + what they'll find when they click)
P.S. line: (optional but often the most-read part — use it for urgency, social proof, or a secondary offer)

---

Sequence brief:
Product/service: [PRODUCT OR SERVICE]
Target subscriber: [WHO IS ON THIS LIST — how they signed up, their awareness level]
Goal of the sequence: [PURCHASE / BOOK A CALL / ACTIVATE FREE TRIAL / ATTEND WEBINAR / OTHER]
Number of emails: [3–7 recommended]
Sending cadence: [e.g., immediately, then every 2 days]
Key selling points: [TOP 3 REASONS SOMEONE SHOULD BUY / SIGN UP]
Main objection to overcome: [THE #1 REASON PEOPLE DON'T BUY]
Offer or incentive: [DISCOUNT / BONUS / GUARANTEE — or "none"]
Brand tone: [Conversational / Professional / Story-driven / Direct]

Sequence structure to follow:
Email 1: Welcome + deliver value (no hard sell)
Email 2: Address the main pain point with a story or insight
Email 3: Introduce the solution and social proof
Email 4 (if included): Handle the main objection directly
Email 5 (if included): Urgency / scarcity / deadline
Final email: Last chance + summary of what they'll miss
```

## Notes
- Plain-text emails often outperform HTML for nurture sequences — keep formatting minimal
- Each email should have one job and one CTA — resist the urge to include multiple links
- Subject lines are 40% of your open rate — always A/B test the two options provided
- Re-engagement email: after the sequence ends, add a "still interested?" email at Day 30 to re-activate non-openers
